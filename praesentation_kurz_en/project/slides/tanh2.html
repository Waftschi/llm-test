Why? Without tanh, c0 and c1 are exactly 3 points apart for every sentence (−1.5 versus −4.5). So every sentence gets 2·3 = 6 wein points, and wein always wins.

Only tanh makes the gap depend on the sentence: sums become switches (on/off), and only switches allow AND and OR.

Without tanh the raw sums go straight into layer 2. Because c0 and c1 are always exactly 3 apart, every sentence gets 6 wein points and the barkeeper always says wein. This holds for any weights: without the bend, two matrices can be multiplied into one, and one matrix can only add up votes. A second benefit of tanh: values stay between minus 1 and plus 1. Large LLMs mostly use GELU or ReLU instead of tanh; the job is the same.
