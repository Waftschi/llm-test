Putting it in context – mini example 1 of 2

The mini net: two switches

Task: “wein” → wein, “bier” → bier, “kein bier” → wein, “kein wein” → bier.

Layer 1: two switches

word points = 3 per word (wein, kein)

c0 = tanh(word points − 1.5)

c1 = tanh(word points − 4.5)

c0 switches on with a single word (OR), c1 only with both (AND).

Layer 2: points for the answer

wein points = 2·c0 − 2·c1

bier points = 1

Whoever has more points wins.

Worked example “kein wein”

1. Word points

wein 3 + kein 3

= 6

→

2. Before tanh

c0: 6 − 1.5 = 4.5

c1: 6 − 4.5 = 1.5

→

3. After tanh

c0 = 1.00

c1 = 0.91

→

4. Answer

wein: 2·1.00 − 2·0.91 = 0.19

0.19 < 1 → bier ✓

tanh squeezes big values to at most 1. So for “kein wein” c0 and c1 end up close together and the wein points become small. That is exactly what should happen.

The mini net only has two words, wein and kein. Each word gives 3 points. Switch c0 subtracts 1.5, so it turns on with a single word, an OR. Switch c1 subtracts 4.5 and only turns on with both words, an AND. Then comes tanh. In layer 2, wein gets 2 times c0 minus 2 times c1 points, bier always 1 point. Example kein wein: 6 word points, c0 before 4.5, c1 before 1.5, after tanh 1.00 and 0.91. The wein points are only 0.19, less than 1, so bier wins.
