Worked example: who carries how much blame?

Mini network with 3 answers (gruss, bier, wein) and 2 concepts (c0, c1). The right answer is bier, the network guesses wein.

1

How wrong was each answer?

D2 = P − Y. P is the guess, Y the right answer (bier = 1).

AnswerP (guess)Y (right)D2 = P − Y
gruss0.130+0.13
bier0.261−0.74
wein0.610+0.61

2

How much blame does each concept carry?

Every answer sends its error back through the weights W2. Then everything is added up.

Conceptgruss +0.13bier −0.74wein +0.61Σ
c0× 0.1 = +0.01× 0.5 = −0.37× 1.5 = +0.92+0.56
c1× 0.2 = +0.03× −0.5 = +0.37× −0.4 = −0.24+0.15

How movable is the concept?

Times (1 − h²): a concept near +1 or −1 hardly changes, one near 0 changes a lot. Result: D1.

Both concepts get the blame 0.11. From that the learning rule works out how the weights in W1 change.

A tiny example with three answers and two concepts. The network guesses wein with 61 percent, the right answer would be bier. Step 1: D2 is guess minus right answer. Bier is far too weak at minus 0.74, wein far too strong at plus 0.61. Step 2: the error of each answer is multiplied by the weight W2 of the concept and added up. Concept 0 votes strongly for wein with weight 1.5 and therefore gets a lot of blame, plus 0.56. Step 3: the factor 1 minus h squared is the slope of tanh. Concept 0 has activation 0.9 and sits almost at the limit, so the factor is only 0.19. Concept 1 is in the middle at minus 0.5, the factor is 0.75. In the end both concepts carry the same blame, 0.11. From that the changes of the weights are calculated.
