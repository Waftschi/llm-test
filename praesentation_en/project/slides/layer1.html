Weight

How strongly a word pushes this concept up (+) or holds it back (−). Every concept has its own weight for every word. It starts as a random number and is learned step by step during training.

Bias

The basic tendency of the concept, even if no word fits. It is a weight whose input is always 1. It starts at 0 and is learned during training as well.

Why tanh?

tanh squeezes z to −1 … +1. From about ±2 the concept is almost fully on or off. Without this bend, two layers would be just one matrix. The result h = tanh(z) = +0.96 (bottom left) goes into layer 2.

Here is a real concept (no. 15) from the model. On the left are the weights of the words in the sentence: “bier” pushes the concept up with +1.23, “kein” even more with +1.82, and the context “[wein]” pulls it down with −0.85. So this concept roughly asks: does kein occur together with bier? On the right, where they come from: weights and bias are not calculated but learned. Both start with random numbers or 0 and are corrected step by step over 1500 training rounds, each time a small step against the error. The bias is technically a weight whose input is always 1, so it is learned with the same rule. It means the tendency without any input. Everything together gives 1.96, and tanh turns that into 0.96. That is the value h of this concept, it is in the last line on the left. tanh matters for two reasons: it limits the values to between minus 1 and plus 1, so no concept can drown out all the others. And it is curved: without this bend, layer 1 and layer 2 together would mathematically be just one single matrix, and the network could not learn combinations like kein plus bier.
