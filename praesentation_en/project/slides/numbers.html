Step 1

Text becomes numbers

Every known word has its own column. If it occurs in the sentence there is a 1, otherwise a 0. The context counts like a word.

hallohibierweinkein[start][wein][bier]
00101010

Example: “kein bier” (no beer) in context [wein], eight of 141 columns shown, three of them with 1. Unknown words are ignored; before learning, “kein” would have been one of them.

bier

The word in the sentence

The guest has just said it, e.g. “kein bier”.

[bier]

The context (state of the conversation)

The guest has already ordered a beer. That is not in the sentence, e.g. for “noch eins” (one more).

For the network these are two separate columns with their own weights. It does not know that they belong together.

The difference: “bier” without brackets is a word the guest has just said. “[bier]” in square brackets is the context, the state of the conversation: the guest has already ordered a beer earlier. That is why the same sentence “noch eins” (one more) can mean another beer in the context [bier], and “you have not ordered anything yet” in the context [start]. For the network “bier” and “[bier]” are two completely separate columns, it does not know that they belong together. Here we see “kein bier” in the context [wein]: the columns kein, bier and [wein] have a 1. The network can only calculate with numbers, so every word gets its own column. In real LLMs, embeddings replace this simple 0/1 representation.
