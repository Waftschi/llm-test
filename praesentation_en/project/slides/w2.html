All of W2: 34 rows (answers) × 24 columns (concepts) = 816 numbers. b2: 34 numbers, one per answer.

W2

One row per answer, one column per concept. The number says how strongly this concept votes for this answer (+) or against it (−).

b2

The basic leaning of an answer that applies no matter which concepts fire. It is added to the sum of the votes.

Example wein

c2 has h = −1.00 and the weight −3.31 for wein. Vote: −3.31 × −1.00 = +3.31. All votes plus b2 (+0.11) give the score +11.4.

On the previous slide you saw the votes of the concepts. Here are the numbers behind them: W2 and b2, the learned numbers of layer 2. W2 is a table with one row per answer and one column per concept, 34 by 24 for us, so 816 numbers. A number says how strongly the concept votes for or against the answer. The vote is weight times activation h. b2 is the bias, one number per answer, the basic leaning independent of the sentence. The calculation is s equals W2 times h plus b2, and the result is the 34 scores. Like W1 and b1, W2 and b2 are learned in training; together there are 4258 numbers in the whole network. The excerpt shows real values from the current model.
