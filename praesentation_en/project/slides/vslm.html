What carries over

The principle stays the same: weighted sums, activation, softmax, measure the error, improve the weights. Only the input (embeddings and position) and the output (vocabulary instead of 34 answers) change.

Our barkeeper is a classifier: it maps a sentence to one of 34 fixed answers and knows no word order. A language model answers a different question, namely which word comes next, and produces text word by word. The principle carries over: you could rebuild our network by putting the output layer on all known words and training it to predict the next word from the previous words. The advantage: you need no hand-labelled examples, the text itself provides the correct answer. With 230 examples the result would hardly be usable, though; real language models need billions of weights and trillions of words.
